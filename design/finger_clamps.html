Finger C-Clamp Set

Twelve TPU clamps, three on each finger. Each one hugs about 70% of the finger with the gap on the palm side, so it snaps on. A strip of flat elastic runs along the back of the finger and presses onto a stud on every clamp, like a watch strap, so the clamps stay linked and never dangle. The fishing line for the motor runs inside the clamps as a U-loop: up one side of the finger, round the tip clamp, and back down the other side.

Press on, peel off, no tools

Each clamp has a small mushroom-shaped stud on top. The elastic has a punched hole for each stud. The domed head stretches the hole open as you press, and the hole closes back under the head's lip so it holds. Pick Strap demo in the 3D view above to watch the whole finger.

1 · Line up and press. Put the hole over the stud and push down with a thumb. The sloped head guides the hole open.
2 · Locked. The elastic sits flat in the shallow recess, trapped under the head. It can't slide along the clamp or lift off when the finger bends.
3 · Peel off. Lift the end of the strap at the fingertip and peel it back towards the hand. Each hole slips up over its head.

What one clamp looks like end-on

The opening is a little narrower than the finger, so the TPU flexes open as it goes on and then grips. The tips of the C are rounded so they don't dig into the palm side of the finger, and both ends of the clamp are flared slightly on the inside.

Strap choice

Why flat elastic, not a printed TPU zigzag

  • It has to stretch a lot. When the hand closes, the back of each finger gets roughly 10–15 mm longer at every joint. Flat elastic stretches that far easily. A printed TPU spring would fight the bend and crack after repeated sessions.
  • You can tune it without reprinting. Swap to a stiffer or softer band, or shorten it so it gives a gentle pull toward open.
  • Nothing dangles. Each clamp has a stud that goes through a punched hole in the elastic, so a clamp can't slide along the strap or fall off between joints.
  • Cheap and washable. One roll of 8 mm elastic covers the whole team, and a worn strip takes two minutes to replace.
Cable routing · top view of one finger

The U-loop

  • Pulls the finger straight. One centre line can pull a little off to one side and let the clamps tip over. Two lines, one on each side, pull evenly, so the finger lifts straight without twisting.
  • Balances itself. The line slides round the groove, so if one side goes slack the other side takes up the difference.
  • No knot at the fingertip. The old design relied on a knot in the tip clamp, and braided line can slip out of a knot. Now the pull is spread round the curved nose.
  • Force stays the same unless you anchor one end. With both ends joined to the motor line, the fingertip gets the same pull as with one line, and the motor reels in the same length of line. To double the force at the fingertip, anchor one end to the back of the hand and pull only the other. The motor then has to reel in twice as much line.

Assembly

Putting one finger together

  1. Print the clampsStand each clamp on its wider end (the knuckle side). No supports needed.
  2. Cut and punch the elasticCut 8 mm flat elastic to the length in the table. Punch three 3 mm holes at the spacings shown, using a leather hole punch. If the elastic is polyester, run a lighter flame past each hole quickly so the edges don't fray.
  3. Fit the clampsSnap each clamp on from the palm side, centred on its segment.
  4. Add the fishing lineFrom the knuckle end, thread the line up the left channels of all three clamps. Hook it round the groove on the tip clamp's nose, then thread it back down the right channels.
  5. Join the two endsAbout 20 mm behind the base clamp, tie both ends together onto the single line that goes into the sheath to the motor. With the finger straight, pull gently and check the line slides round the nose so both sides go equally tight.
  6. Slide in the flex sensorPush the sensor, pins towards the hand, through the flat slot under the line channels: base clamp first, then the middle clamp. Fix only the pin end, on the back of the hand. The rest must be free to slide as the finger bends.
  7. Press the strap onWith the finger straight, press the first hole onto the base clamp's stud, then the middle, then the tip. The elastic should lie flat and snug, not stretched.

Check on the first test fit

If the strap is too stiff to press on, make the holes 3.5 mm. If it pops off by itself when the hand closes, punch 2.5 mm holes or ask for a bigger stud head. If a clamp feels too tight or loose, the width can change in 0.5 mm steps and the set regenerated. For a tightly clenched fist, the palm-side opening may be hard to reach; try sliding the clamps on lengthwise from the fingertip on the test hand before committing.
